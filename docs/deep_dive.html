Context & Conclusion

Children in conflict-affected regions of North-East Nigeria—particularly Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) states—face severe and uneven barriers to education access. Armed conflict trigged by Boko Haram which means Western Education is prohibited in Hausa has damaged school infrastructure, displaced millions of people, and created persistent insecurity that limits safe access to existing schools.

While education actors, including EBI, maintain strong field presence and local relationships, decision-making about where to prioritise interventions remains constrained by fragmented information flows and limited system-wide visibility.
This dashboard provides systemic, data-driven analysis at a glance to suggest priority intervention zones.
It also highlights when armed conflict can occur and where it is highly likely to occur.

Methodology Notes

  • Education Access Gap: Calculated by dividing the 2022 LGA population projections by the number of currently operational schools in that LGA.
  • Predictive Risk Score: Derived from conflict event data (2020-2024). The formula heavily weights recent momentum (attacks in the last 18 months count double) and overall intensity (adding a fraction of total fatalities) to historical frequency.
  • Holiday Trends: Analyzed using a 7-day window (3 days prior, the day of, and 3 days post-event) around major recognized public and religious holidays in Nigeria to identify tactical spikes.

Data Sources

  • School Locations & Coordinates: GRID3 (Geo-Referenced Infrastructure and Demographic Data for Development), circa 2018-2020.
  • School Operational Status: iMMAP / Nigeria Education Cluster, "North East Nigeria School List", Status as of June 2019. (Available via Humanitarian Data Exchange).
  • Population Data: National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) & National Population Commission (NPC), 2022 LGA Population Projections.
  • Conflict Data: ACLED (Armed Conflict Location & Event Data Project) / UCDP (Uppsala Conflict Data Program). Filtered for events occurring between January 1, 2020, and December 31, 2024.
  • IDP Locations: IOM DTM (Displacement Tracking Matrix) Nigeria, Site Assessment Round 50, April 2026.
